## Section A – Scenario-Based Multiple Choice (30 × 2 marks = 60 marks)

Select the best answer (A–D) for each scenario.

1. During a safety briefing, Tahlia has already signed out goggles but removed them while sweeping because she is “only cleaning.” Her classmates are using chisels nearby. What should Tahlia do next?  
   A. Keep sweeping but stand further away.  
   B. Put her goggles back on immediately.  
   C. Ask the teacher to move the others.  
   D. Finish sweeping quickly with her head turned.

2. While packing up, Rowan leaves clamps on the floor to “grab later” and begins sweeping around them. Another student is carrying a sharp chisel past his bench. What is the safest action Rowan should take?  
   A. Leave the clamps; the floor will be cleared soon.  
   B. Ask the other student to walk around.  
   C. Stop sweeping and store the clamps before anyone trips.  
   D. Slide the clamps under the bench with his foot.

3. Mia has planed one face of her board flat and marked it “F.” She forgets to identify a face edge before laying out dovetail tails and ends up measuring from a rough edge. What risk has Mia introduced?  
   A. Her saw teeth will dull more quickly.  
   B. The joint may be over-strengthened.  
   C. Reference measurements may be inaccurate, causing gaps.  
   D. The glue will not bond on the rough edge.

4. Karim needs to scribe a series of parallel layout lines 6 mm in from the edge on all four sides of a panel. Which tool best suits this task?  
   A. Sliding bevel  
   B. Coping saw  
   C. Marking gauge  
   D. Dowel jig

5. While researching traditional woodworking, Sienna reads about a tool that uses spinifex resin to secure a stone blade to a wooden handle. Which practice is she studying?  
   A. Bark canoe stripping  
   B. Hafting in First Nations toolmaking  
   C. Fire-straightening spear shafts  
   D. Steam bending coolamons

6. Jayden wants to recreate a lightweight shield inspired by Northern Australian traditions and selects heavy ironbark because it “looks tough.” Which decision better honours the original technology?  
   A. Use fig tree root for a lighter, resilient shield.  
   B. Switch to laser-cut plywood for accuracy.  
   C. Keep ironbark but sand thinner.  
   D. Replace timber with recycled plastic.

7. A client needs a toolbox that will survive rough site use without relying entirely on glue. Which joint should Asha recommend for the corners?  
   A. Biscuit joint  
   B. Butt joint with screws  
   C. Sliding dovetail joint  
   D. Box-pin joint cut with a band saw

8. During marking out, Oscar strikes a knot in his Radiata pine side panel. The saw suddenly binds and wanders. What is the most appropriate response?  
   A. Force the saw through quickly.  
   B. Pause, re-mark, and use a sharper saw with slow strokes through the knot.  
   C. Switch to hardwood for the whole project.  
   D. Apply more glue later to fill the gap.

9. In a factory simulation, students compare methods of measuring 50 identical drawer sides. Which statement describes industrial absolute measurement?  
   A. Each part is traced from a master component.  
   B. Dimensions are taken from whichever edge looks straightest.  
   C. Every part is cut to exact digital measurements so pieces interchange.  
   D. Workers adjust measurements after test fitting.

10. Linh must produce 40 identical handle recesses. What approach saves time and maintains accuracy?  
    A. Freehand routing each recess.  
    B. Building a jig to guide the router repeatedly.  
    C. Cutting once and tracing around it.  
    D. Switching to hand chisels for better feel.

11. A small business wants to repeat the class carry-all using a CNC router. Which preparation step is essential before machining?  
    A. Sanding all boards to 240 grit.  
    B. Writing G-code directly on the control panel without a plan.  
    C. Creating or importing an accurate CAD file for the machine to follow.  
    D. Running the machine empty to “warm it up.”

12. Priya enjoys fine finishing work and wants to install custom cabinetry in homes. Which timber career aligns best with her interests?  
    A. Site carpenter  
    B. Wood machinist  
    C. Cabinetmaker  
    D. Saw doctor

13. Elijah completes a 3D model of the carry-all in CAD. What must he do before the CNC router can cut the parts?  
    A. Upload photos of the model to the router.  
    B. Export the model to CAM software to generate toolpaths.  
    C. Replace timber with acrylic.

14. An architect proposes using engineered timber beams for a school project to reduce steel usage. Which material supports that goal?  
    A. Plain Radiata pine studs  
    B. MDF sheet  
    C. Glulam fabricated from finger-jointed lamellae

15. A class aims to cut waste by reusing offcuts. Which practice reflects sustainable thinking from Week 6?  
    A. Burning offcuts to heat the workshop.  
    B. Storing offcuts in bins for smaller components and prototypes.  
    C. Leaving offcuts on benches for anyone to take.

16. During glue-up, Ava notices a finger joint slipping out of alignment. What should she do first?  
    A. Hammer the joint deeper.

17. Mateo plans to screw metal handles directly into the side panels without drilling, to save time. What is the likely consequence?

18. Zoe dry-fits her carry-all and sees a 3 mm gap at one corner. What should she do before gluing?

19. When checking a rectangular base, Thomas finds the diagonal measurements differ by 4 mm. What does this indicate?  
    A. The base is perfectly flat.  
    B. The base is square.

20. Lara begins finishing with 240-grit paper straight away because it “feels smooth.” What issue might arise?

21. Carlos spots a shallow dent after sanding. Which solution aligns with Week 9 guidance?

26. Lucia applies stain, waits an hour, and brushes on a heavy coat of varnish without sanding. The surface dries rough. What step did she skip?

27. During finishing, Connor chooses an oil-based polyurethane in a closed room because “it’ll dry faster.” Which safety control should he have added?

## Section B – Short Answer (5 × 4 marks = 20 marks)

Answer each question in 2–4 well-structured sentences. Support responses with relevant terminology.

1. **Community Knowledge and Materials**  
   During a design investigation, your team discovers local Community protocols for sustainably harvesting timber. Explain how you would incorporate two of these principles into the planning and construction of a new carry-all prototype.

2. **Adhesives & Clamping Strategy**  
   A classmate asks why their glued box-pin joint opened overnight. Describe the correct clamping sequence and curing considerations that would prevent this from happening again.

3. **Assembly Troubleshooting**  
   In a dry fit you detect a 2 mm twist across the base. Outline the steps you would take to correct the twist before gluing, including measurement checks.

4. **Surface Preparation Plan**  
   You must deliver a carry-all with a stain-and-varnish finish. Summarise the sanding, staining, and varnishing sequence you will follow to achieve a professional result.

5. **Quality Assurance Documentation**  
   You are preparing evidence for assessment. List the key data and reflections you will record to demonstrate the carry-all meets functional and aesthetic requirements.

### Section B Suggested Points

1. Reference to respectful harvesting (permissions, seasonal timing), full utilisation of timber, minimal waste, cultural acknowledgement in documentation.  
2. Even glue coverage, correct joint fit before glue, immediate clamping with pads, leaving under pressure for full cure time, avoiding premature handling.  
3. Re-check face references, plane or sand high corners, adjust clamp pressure diagonally, confirm equal diagonals before final assembly.  
4. Progressive sanding (120→180→240), dust removal, even stain application with wipe-off, test on scrap, thin varnish coats with 320–400 grit de-nibbing.  
5. Record measurements (diagonals, joint gaps), hardware alignment, finish type/coats, functional tests, reflective notes on improvements.
